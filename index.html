THE MODEL BEHIND THE IMAGE

Light follows spacetime.

This is a numerical Schwarzschild ray tracer, with a simplified, stationary thin accretion disk. Every color mode uses the same computed light paths.

01 — Curved spacetime & lightds² = −f dt² + dr²/f + r² dΩ² · f = 1 − rₛ/r
u″ + u = 3Mu² · u = 1/r

Each pixel traces a null geodesic backward using adaptive fourth-order Runge–Kutta. The invariant u′² + u² − 2Mu³ = 1/b² supplies a numerical check. A captured ray contributes no background light.

02 — Three radii, three different meaningsrₛ = 2GM/c² · rph = 1.5rₛ · rISCO = 3rₛ

The horizon is the absorbing boundary. The photon sphere contains unstable circular light orbits, but does not glow by itself. The distant apparent critical radius is bcrit = √27 rₛ/2 ≈ 2.598rₛ. ISCO is the inner edge of our stable, orbiting disk. Finite-distance angular size is computed separately.

03 — Temperature & visible lightT⁴ ∝ r⁻³ [1 − √(rISCO/r)]
Bλ(T) = 2hc² / {λ⁵ [exp(hc/λkT) − 1]}

The zero-torque Shakura–Sunyaev profile peaks at 49rISCO/36 and vanishes exactly at ISCO. Peak temperature sets the accretion-rate normalization. Natural color integrates Planck radiation over 380–780 nm using approximate CIE 1931 functions, then maps XYZ to sRGB with exposure and a filmic curve. Hot disks peak in UV or X-rays but can still emit strongly in visible light.

04 — Doppler beaming & gravitational shiftD = 1 / [γ(1 − β · n)]
g = D × √(fem/fobs) × gobserver
Iν,obs = g³ Iν,em(ν/g) · Ibol,obs = g⁴ Ibol,em

The approaching disk is brighter and blueshifted. Frequency shift, photon arrival rate and angular beaming combine in the intensity transformation. Spectral intensity uses g³ and bolometric intensity uses g⁴; those powers are not an optical-depth switch. For a blackbody, evaluating the spectrum at Tobs = gTem already includes the boost. The observer can be below the emitter, in which case gravity produces a blueshift.

05 — Moving observersβlocal = √[M/(r − 2M)]
Ω = √(M/r³) · dt/dτ = 1/√(1 − 3M/r)

The orbiting camera follows a circular geodesic in a fixed orbital plane. A Lorentz boost in the local orthonormal tetrad changes ray directions and received frequencies. Circular mode stays outside ISCO; the static camera can approach 1.51rₛ. The clock multiplier accelerates physical proper time. Animated camera repositioning is a navigation transition, not a free-fall trajectory.

06 — Gravity maps & switchesz = 1/√(1 − rₛ/r) − 1
Φ/c² = −rₛ/(2r) · K rₛ⁴ = 12(rₛ/r)⁶

The overlay samples emission radius on the disk and closest approach on background rays. It is an educational scalar wash. Turning disk Doppler or gravity shift off sets the corresponding frequency factor to one. Higher-order light off discards wound image contributions; sky lensing off uses unbent sky directions. These switches deliberately break parts of the physical image for comparison.

07 — Accuracy & browser limits

Geometry follows exact Schwarzschild equations, solved to finite numerical accuracy. Approximations include the Newtonian disk temperature profile, zero disk thickness, a synthetic sky with finite-size stars, CIE fitting, finite pixels, and a bounded RK4 budget. Single-precision GPU arithmetic limits very high-order sub-rings; the diagnostics switch marks unresolved rays magenta. A double-precision numerical lookup accelerates eligible weak-field sky rays.

No time-coordinate integration is needed for this stationary scene: u(φ) remains regular at the horizon. There is no horizon-crossing plunge, Kerr spin, plasma scattering, GRMHD, or DNGR-style beam tracing. Performance depends on the GPU and selected resolution.
